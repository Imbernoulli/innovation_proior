## Research question

In lambda calculus, beta-reduction treats a single function application as a single substitution. A term often contains multiple reducible positions, so the order of execution is not unique. If different orders produce different final terms, lambda calculus can only describe some particular evaluation strategy rather than serve as a stable equational theory of computation. The question is: given multiple reduction paths starting from the same term, can their eventual results be reconciled?

## Background

Viewed as an abstract rewriting system, lambda calculus has terms as nodes and single-step reductions as directed edges. A computation is no longer just a linear trajectory but a walk through a graph generated by the reduction relation.

The picture of confluence is a generalization of the diamond property: if `M ->* N1` and `M ->* N2`, then there exists some `P` such that `N1 ->* P` and `N2 ->* P`. Here `->*` denotes zero or more steps of reduction.

## Evaluation setting

What is being evaluated is not an experimental metric but whether the conceptual explanation captures the theorem's structural role. The minimum requirement is to make three things clear: first, that the branching of beta-reduction paths comes from the choice of redex; second, the definition of confluence and the constraint it places on reduction paths; third, the relationship between normal form and reduction order.
