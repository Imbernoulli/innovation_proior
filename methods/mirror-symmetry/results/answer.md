# Mirror Symmetry

The distinctive insight of mirror symmetry is exchanging the complex moduli and the complexified Kahler moduli of a pair of Calabi-Yau manifolds. That is, the A-model on `X`, governed by the Kahler parameters, can be computed via the B-model on the mirror `X^vee`, governed by the complex-structure parameters.

This step turns string duality into a computational principle for enumerative geometry. Originally, on `X`, one had to count rational curves or compute Gromov-Witten invariants; treated directly, these problems run into the moduli space of curves, singularities, compactification, and virtual counting. After passing to the mirror, the same physical quantity appears on `X^vee` as the period integral of the holomorphic three-form:

`int_gamma Omega`

These periods vary with the complex structure, forming a variation of Hodge structures, and satisfy Picard-Fuchs differential equations. The computational route thus goes from "directly counting curves" to "solve the period equation, find the mirror map, expand the Yukawa coupling or the prepotential." The enumerative invariants on the original Calabi-Yau can be read off from the expansion coefficients.

The methodological breakthrough is that mirror symmetry does not patch the hard counting problem locally into something computable; instead it changes how the problem is expressed via dual geometry. The quantum enumerative data on the A-model side is encoded, on the B-model side, by Hodge theory and period computations. The duality in physics therefore does more than show two theories are equivalent — it can predict concrete enumerative-geometry integers.

The most iconic historical signal is the quintic threefold: the mirror computation of Candelas, de la Ossa, Green, and Parkes gave strong predictions for the counts of rational curves, which later drove the rigorous mathematical development of Gromov-Witten theory, the mirror theorem, and homological mirror symmetry. It demonstrates not a one-off trick but a new mathematical strategy: translate a hard-to-compute geometric object into a dual space where the computable structure becomes visible.
