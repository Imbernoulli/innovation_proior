# Sandpile Relief Under a Firing Budget

## Problem
You are given a **rooted sandpile**: a simple graph on vertices `0..n-1` (the *piles*) plus one
special **sink** vertex `n` that never holds chips. Every edge has an integer weight (its
*capacity*). The **degree** `deg(v)` of a pile `v` is the sum of the weights of its incident
edges. Pile `v` starts holding `h_v` chips.

A pile `v` is **unstable** if its current chip count is `>= deg(v)`. **Firing** an unstable pile
`v` removes `deg(v)` chips from it and sends, along every incident edge `(v,u)` of weight `w`,
exactly `w` chips to `u` (chips reaching the sink vanish forever). This is the classical
*abelian sandpile* rule: **whether a legal firing sequence stabilizes the graph, and how many
times each pile fires while doing so, never depends on the order the piles were fired in.** Two
different legal firing orders that fire each pile the same total number of times always leave
every pile at the exact same final chip count. So you never have to plan an order at all — only
the total number of times each pile fires matters.

You also have a firing **budget** `K`: the total number of individual pile-firings you may spend,
summed over every pile. You do not have to spend it all, and you do not have to reach a fully
stable graph — some piles may remain unstable when you stop.

## Input
```
n m K
a_1 b_1 w_1
...
a_m b_m w_m
h_0 h_1 ... h_{n-1}
c_0 c_1 ... c_{n-1}
```
Vertices `0..n-1` are piles, vertex `n` is the sink. Each of the `m` edge lines gives an
undirected edge `(a,b)` of weight `w` (`0<=a,b<=n`, `a != b`). `h_v` is pile `v`'s starting chip
count and `c_v` is its **criticality weight** (how expensive it is to leave `v` unstable). Every
pile has `deg(v) >= 1`.

## Output
Print `n` non-negative integers `f_0 ... f_{n-1}`: the total number of times each pile fires.

## Feasibility
`sum f_v <= K`, and `f` must be **realizable**: some order of individual legal firings (each
firing a currently-unstable pile) must fire pile `v` exactly `f_v` times in total, for every `v`.
(By the fact above, if such an order exists, every such order reaches the same final chip
counts, so any single way of checking this is conclusive.) Any other output is infeasible and
scores 0.

## Objective (minimize)
Apply `f` to reach final chip counts. A pile `v` is still unstable if its final count is
`>= deg(v)`; its leftover excess is `e_v = max(0, final_count_v - deg(v) + 1)`. Minimize
```
R = sum_v c_v * e_v
```
the total criticality-weighted instability left behind.

## Scoring
Let `B` be `R` for the do-nothing output (`f = 0`, i.e. the input's own instability). Your ratio
is `B / (B + 9*R)`. Doing nothing scores exactly `0.1`; driving `R` toward `0` pushes the ratio
toward `1`. Ratios are averaged over 10 hidden cases. An infeasible output scores 0.

## Constraints
`1 <= n <= 2000`, `1 <= m <= 4000`, `0 <= K <= 4*10^6`, `1 <= w <= 60` (edge weight),
`0 <= h_v <= 3*10^5`, `1 <= c_v <= 25`. Time limit: 4 s. Memory limit: 512 MB.

## Example
Input:
```
3 3 6
0 3 2
1 3 40
2 3 40
600 70 70
3 10 10
```
Pile 0: `deg=2`, `h=600`, so `e_0 = 599`, cost `c_0*e_0 = 1797` if left alone. Piles 1 and 2:
`deg=40`, `h=70` each, so `e=31`, cost `10*31=310` each; firing either just once drops it to
`70-40=30 < 40` (fully stable). `B = 1797+310+310 = 2417`.

With budget `K=6`: fire piles 1 and 2 once each (cost 2, fully removes their `310+310=620`
penalty), then spend the remaining 4 firings on pile 0: `height_0 = 600-4*2=592`, `e_0=591`,
penalty `3*591=1773`. `R = 1773`, ratio `= 2417/(2417+9*1773) = 2417/18374 ~= 0.132`.

Firing pile 0 all six times instead (the pile with the biggest raw chip count, ignoring that
piles 1 and 2 are far cheaper to finish) leaves `height_0=588`, `e_0=587`, penalty `1761`, and
piles 1, 2 fully untouched at `310` each: `R = 1761+310+310 = 2381`, ratio `~= 0.101` — almost no
better than doing nothing at all.
