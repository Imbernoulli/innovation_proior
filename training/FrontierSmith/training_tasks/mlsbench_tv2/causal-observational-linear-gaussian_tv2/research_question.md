The directed half of the score is where classical pipelines quietly give up. A skeleton with
every endpoint left as a tail earns nothing on arrow precision or arrow recall, and each
arrowhead that faithfulness would license but the method declines to draw is a point SHD keeps
charging for. This variant makes orientation the primary objective: the deliverable is not a
good adjacency structure that happens to carry a few arrows, but a defensible set of directed
claims layered on whatever skeleton the data supports.

The mechanism under study is the two-stage logic of equivalence-class learning taken seriously
as the object of optimization. Unshielded colliders are the only place observational Gaussian
data speaks directly about direction, and Meek's propagation rules are the only sound way to
spread that signal; so every arrowhead in the output must trace back to a detected v-structure
or to a propagation step licensed by one. Arrowheads placed by decree — degree heuristics,
lexical order, per-scenario switches — are outside the rules of this variant, as is any change
to what the harness measures.

Three commitments define success. First, arrow precision must stay high enough that the
directed subgraph can be read as evidence, not guesswork: a wrong arrowhead is worse here than
a missing one. Second, arrow recall must grow with the reliability of collider detection, and
that detector has to survive the noisy 400-sample regime where conditional independence tests
are at their weakest. Third, adjacency quality may not be sacrificed to manufacture
orientation opportunities; the skeleton is the substrate, and corrupting it to mine colliders
is self-defeating. The claim to defend: on these five regimes, careful collider identification
plus sound propagation orients a substantial fraction of the equivalence class without
surrendering the precision that makes those orientations worth reporting.
