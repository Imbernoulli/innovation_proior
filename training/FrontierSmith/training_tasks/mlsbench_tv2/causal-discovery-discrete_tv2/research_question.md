Skeleton recovery on the bnlearn suite is the mature half of the problem;
the direction channel is where methods quietly bleed. A procedure can post
respectable adjacency numbers on every network and still surrender a third
of that quality when its arrows are checked, because collider detection
rides on fragile conditional-independence calls and propagation rules
amplify every early mistake. Directions touch three of the five reported
terms per network: arrow precision, arrow recall, and SHD (which charges a
mis-oriented edge as an error even when the adjacency is right). An output
that hedges by leaving everything undirected caps arrow recall at zero and
concedes that entire channel.

This variant treats orientation as the deliverable rather than a
finishing step. The concrete target is two-fold. First, close the gap
between adjacency precision and arrow precision on each network: an arrow
you commit to should be as trustworthy as an adjacency you commit to,
which means v-structures certified by explicit conditioning-set evidence,
propagation only from certified colliders, and a principled arbitration
rule (score-based or otherwise) when constraint evidence is ambiguous.
Second, under that trust discipline, push arrow recall as far as the data
allows — every edge that the true equivalence class directs but your
output leaves undirected is arrow recall you chose not to collect.
Keeping a pair unoriented stays an available answer — and for edges the
true equivalence class itself declines to direct, the correct one — but
it must be a deliberate finding, not a blanket policy.

Defend the following with the harness's own numbers, produced by one
fixed procedure across Cancer, Child, Alarm, Hailfinder, and Win95pts:
arrow precision within a small margin of adjacency precision on every
network, arrow recall clearly above what a direction-free output would
score, and SHD no worse than a comparable undirected submission — i.e.
the orientations pay for themselves.
