Thirty validation lookups are the entire information income of a run; everything else —
arithmetic over architectures already paid for, structural reasoning about the six-edge cell,
bookkeeping of any size — is free in unlimited quantity. This variant frames the search as
procurement: an oracle call is a purchase, and the design object is the purchasing rule.
Before any cell reaches the oracle it should pass a free appraisal built from the ledger of
prior purchases; a candidate whose neighbourhood evidence already argues it is mediocre should
be rejected at zero cost and never bought, and a cell that has been bought once must never be
bought again, because the harness charges repeat lookups at full price.

What makes this a research brief rather than bookkeeping is the tension inside the clearing
bar. Set it harsh and spending concentrates on look-alikes of the current leader — cheap
confirmation, no discovery. Slacken it and the appraisal stops mattering at all, leaving a
shopper who buys whatever turns up. The
bar, the appraisal function, and the explicit conditions under which an evidence-free
(exploratory) purchase is worth full price are the contribution; the placeholder's crude
best-neighbour estimate marks the spot where a better appraiser belongs.

The scoreboard does not pay for thrift itself: what is recorded per dataset is the test
accuracy of the one cell nominated at the end, across the five seeded repetitions of the run.
Frugality has to cash out as accuracy — the argument being that thirty well-gated purchases
put the final nomination higher than thirty impulsive ones could, on all three image
benchmarks, from the forgiving one to the brutal one. Defend the mechanism with the ledger:
which candidates were refused, what the refusals saved, and where the spared queries went
instead.
