Non-Gaussian noise makes the full DAG identifiable in principle, but the metrics of this benchmark expose a tension the classical estimators leave unresolved: F1 rewards reporting edges, SHD punishes every one you get wrong, and a directed edge is credited only when skeleton and orientation are both correct. Estimators that chase recall flood the graph and lose precision and SHD together; on the hundred-node scale-free setting — where a thousand samples must adjudicate roughly ten thousand ordered pairs and hub nodes concentrate the correlation mass — this failure mode is the norm, and the geometric mean over the three settings makes that setting the binding constraint.

This variant asks for precision-governed directed recovery: every edge you emit should carry its own directional evidence extracted from non-Gaussianity, and an edge whose direction you cannot support is an edge you do not report. The operating discipline is twofold. First, a reporting budget that scales with the number of variables rather than with the number of correlated pairs, so hub-heavy graphs cannot flood the output. Second, an explicit orientation statistic whose magnitude functions as the admission ticket for each edge. Design that statistic so it does not presuppose a noise family: the three settings draw noise from Laplace, exponential, and uniform distributions — super-Gaussian, skewed, and sub-Gaussian — and a cumulant sign convention tuned to one family will invert on another. One fixed configuration must serve all three; noise type, graph family, and density are unknown to the method at run time.

Success is measured exactly as the harness measures it — directed-edge F1, precision, recall, and SHD per setting — with the specific demand that precision and SHD improve together rather than trading against each other, and that the hundred-node setting stops being the floor of the multiplicative aggregate.
