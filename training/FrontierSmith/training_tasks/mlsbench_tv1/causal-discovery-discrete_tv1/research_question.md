The five bnlearn benchmarks span a fifteen-fold range of dimensionality — from Cancer's five variables to Win95pts' seventy-six — and the task aggregates the five per-network scores by geometric mean, so the weakest network sets a hard ceiling on the whole task. In practice that weakest network is a large one: procedures that look flawless on Cancer and Child shed adjacency precision and pile up structural errors as the variable count grows and cardinality patterns diversify, because the pool of candidate edges grows quadratically while the true graph stays sparse. Your objective is scale-flat recovery: one procedure, run with one fixed configuration on every dataset, whose per-network quality profile is as level as possible across the size spectrum — concretely, lift Hailfinder and Win95pts without giving back the small and medium networks.

Two disciplines define the variant. First, no oracle scale knowledge: the method may not branch on network identity, node-count thresholds, or a recognized cardinality fingerprint. Whatever adapts must be a statistic computed from the data and used continuously — an association-strength quantile, an effective-sample-size correction, a per-pair degrees-of-freedom adjustment, a reporting budget tied to the number of variables. Second, orientation must be earned: a wrongly claimed arrow is punished twice, once through arrow precision and again through SHD, and on large sparse networks speculative orientation is the dominant error source. Prefer leaving an edge undirected over orienting it on weak evidence; commit to a direction only where a v-structure or a sound propagation rule defends it.

Scoring is unchanged — SHD, adjacency precision/recall, and arrow precision/recall against the ground-truth equivalence class of each network — but the success criterion to design for is the level of the worst network, not the average, because the multiplicative aggregate forgives nothing.
