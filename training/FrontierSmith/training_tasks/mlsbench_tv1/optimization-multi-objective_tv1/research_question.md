Every problem instance here reports three numbers — hypervolume, inverted generational distance, and spread — which are averaged per instance before the instances are combined multiplicatively, so the total is governed by whichever problem-metric pairing your algorithm handles worst. The pairings that fail are well known, and they fail in opposite directions. On a disconnected true front, density machinery built for contiguous fronts piles solutions at segment boundaries: spread deteriorates while hypervolume still looks healthy. On a landscape with many locally optimal fronts, the population commits early to the first front it reaches: the convergence metrics collapse together while the (wrong) front remains beautifully uniform.

This variant asks for survival and variation machinery engineered for those two failure regimes at once — and engineered blind. The harness withholds the problem's identity and its analytic front; your strategy only ever sees individuals with evaluated objective vectors. The admissible adaptation signals are therefore online statistics of the population's own objective space — normalised extents, nearest-neighbour gap structure along the current non-dominated set, the velocity of the ideal point — and they must be consumed as continuous quantities that modulate the algorithm, never as a classifier that dispatches to a different named method per problem.

One code path must serve both two- and three-objective instances at whatever population size and generation count the spec provides; there is no per-problem tuning because there is no problem label to tune on. Treat each instance as scored by the weakest of its three reported metrics: a run that converges but bunches, or that spaces itself evenly along a local front, counts as a failure of the mechanism rather than a partial success.

What must survive scrutiny at the end is the mechanism itself: which measured statistic detected each regime, how the response was graded rather than switched, and why handling one regime did not sacrifice the other.
